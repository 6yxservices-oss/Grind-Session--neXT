# CMO Deck — Speaker Notes & Talking Points
*Use alongside `02-cmo-deck.md`. Total run-time: ~10 minutes for 6 slides + Q&A buffer.*

---

## Before you start

- **Audience:** MVP exec team (CEO, CMO, partnerships lead, head of digital)
- **Goal:** Walk out with a greenlight on the 14-day pilot
- **Energy:** Confident, not technical. You're not pitching a feature — you're pitching an operating model.
- **Open with eye contact, not slides.** Slide 1 is a single line; let it land before clicking forward.

---

## SLIDE 1 — *"Every fight is a 90-day attention spike."*
**Time:** 60 seconds
**On screen:** the headline, the rent-vs-own line.

**Say:**
> "Before I show you anything, one observation. Every MVP fight generates more attention in 90 days than most brands generate in a year. But today, that attention isn't ours. It belongs to Meta, to YouTube, to ESPN. We pay to reach our own fans every single cycle. What I'm going to show you is how we change that — how we turn one fight into a year of revenue, and one sponsor into ten."

**Pause.** Click to slide 2.

**Likely interrupt:** *"What's this going to cost us?"*
**Answer:** "Nothing new. Existing tech, existing team, existing fight calendar. We're going to redeploy hours, not budget. I'll get to the ask on slide 6."

---

## SLIDE 2 — *"Three gaps we live with today"*
**Time:** 90 seconds
**On screen:** the gap table.

**Say:**
> "We have three gaps that cost us money every event. One — anonymous audience: we can't email the people who bought our last PPV, so we re-acquire them at full cost next time. Two — unmeasurable sponsorships: we sell logos for X when the same impression is worth 5X if it comes with data. Three — the audience goes dark between fights, which means we never compound. The platform fixes all three."

**One-liner to land it:**
> "If we can't name the fan, we can't monetize the fan twice."

**Likely interrupt:** *"Don't we already have email lists?"*
**Answer:** "We have lists. We don't have a *system* that captures, scores, and reactivates them tied to engagement. That's the difference between a list and an asset."

---

## SLIDE 3 — *"The Platform, in plain English"*
**Time:** 90 seconds
**On screen:** the engagement + analytics two-layer view.

**Say:**
> "Here's the whole thing in one frame. The fan platform on top — that's the fun: predictions, scorecards, walkout votes, trivia. Underneath is Dropt, the analytics layer that turns every interaction into a row of data we can act on. Engagement on top, intelligence underneath, revenue out the side. That's it. Nothing complicated."

**Demo move (optional, 30 sec):** Pull up the platform on your phone, show "Pick the Round." If the room is technical, this lands harder than any slide.

**Likely interrupt:** *"Will fans actually do this?"*
**Answer:** "Boxing is a *prediction-native* sport. Fans are already doing this in group chats and on Twitter for free. We're just giving them a better place to do it — and capturing the data when they do."

---

## SLIDE 4 — *"Three Revenue Engines"*
**Time:** 2.5 minutes — the most important slide.
**On screen:** the three engines with lift numbers.

**Say:**
> "Three ways this turns into revenue.
>
> **One — sponsorship reinvented.** We stop selling logos and start selling *moments + audiences*. 'Pick the Round, Presented by Celsius.' Celsius gets the activation *and* the audience file. That's a 5–10x CPM uplift over a broadcast logo. Same fight, more revenue.
>
> **Two — direct fan revenue.** Once we have the captured list, we use it to drive PPV, tickets, and merch. Retargeted traffic converts 3–5x cold traffic. We're already paying for the cold traffic — this is upside on the same media spend.
>
> **Three — year-round monetization.** A clean 250K-person fight-fan list, refreshed every event, is worth two to four million a year on its own. Through addressable media for the next fight, sponsor newsletter inventory, and affiliate revenue between fights."

**Land the math:**
> "If we run this through three fights this year and end with a 500K-fan list, that's a new revenue line that compounds. Every fight makes the next one cheaper to fill."

**Likely interrupt:** *"What's the realistic sponsor revenue?"*
**Answer:** "Conservative pilot: $150K from one sponsor on one activation. Stretch goal year one: $2–4M across three fights and one season-exclusive deal."

---

## SLIDE 5 — *"The CMO Operating Model"*
**Time:** 90 seconds
**On screen:** the ratio + Slack digest.

**Say:**
> "I want to make sure this isn't seen as a tech project. It's an operating model. One ratio runs the whole thing — *revenue per captured fan divided by cost per captured fan*. If it's over one, we scale spend. If it's under one, we fix the funnel. That's the conversation in every weekly meeting from now on.
>
> Day to day, three numbers in Slack: fans captured, cost per fan, revenue per fan. Anyone in this room can read that digest in 10 seconds and know if we're winning."

**Land it:**
> "Most marketing dashboards have 40 numbers and zero decisions. This one has three numbers and one decision."

**Likely interrupt:** *"How do we attribute revenue per fan?"*
**Answer:** "Trailing 90-day attributable revenue divided by captured fans. We use a tagged retargeting audience and a holdout group. Imperfect, but directionally sharp enough to make decisions on."

---

## SLIDE 6 — *"The Ask"*
**Time:** 90 seconds — close strong.
**On screen:** pilot scope + investment.

**Say:**
> "Here's what I'm asking for. A 14-day pilot on the next fight. One activation live — Pick the Round. One sponsor pilot sold, even at half-rate, just to prove the model. Pixels firing. One sponsor-ready report exported from Dropt at the end.
>
> No new tech. No new vendors. No new budget. We're repurposing marketing hours we'd already spend. The cost to test is effectively zero. The upside is a new revenue line we own forever."

**Close:**
> "We don't need a bigger fight. We need to keep the fans we already have. Give me 14 days and one fight, and I'll come back with the first sponsor case study and a real number on the table."

**Pause.** Don't fill silence. Let them respond.

**Likely interrupt:** *"What if the pilot doesn't hit?"*
**Answer:** "Then we know in 14 days, we've spent zero new dollars, and we've still built a captured list and a pixel-tagged audience that makes the next campaign cheaper. There is no losing scenario."

## Q&A — anticipated questions & answers

**"Why now?"**
> Because we have the platform built and the analytics stack live. Every fight we run without this is one we can't go back and capture data on. The cost of waiting is real.

**"Can we just buy this audience from Meta?"**
> No. Meta will rent us a *similar* audience for $11–18 a head. We can build *our own* audience for $3–5 a head, and own it forever. Different unit economics, different asset class.

**"What about privacy?"**
> Everything is opt-in, double opt-in for SMS, fully GDPR/CCPA compliant. Legal has reviewed the flows. We capture less data than any sportsbook the same fans already use.

**"Who runs this?"**
> Existing team. Marketing owns activations, partnerships owns sponsor sales, data team owns the dashboard. Nobody is hired for this in the pilot. If it works, we revisit headcount after fight #2.

**"How is this different from what [competitor] does?"**
> Most other promotions sell sponsorship as broadcast inventory. We're selling it as *audience product.* That's the difference, and it's why our CPMs end up 5–10x higher.

**"What if a sponsor wants exclusivity but we haven't sold it yet?"**
> We have three tiers — single activation, fight week, season exclusive. Exclusivity is the season tier and it's priced accordingly. It protects future inventory, not just current.

**"Why should I trust the projections?"**
> Don't trust them — pilot them. That's the whole point of the 14-day ask. We'll have real numbers, not projections, before we ask for a real budget.
